# Hero ↔ Villain Conflict Models — NOT CANON

**Creator particularly likes options 1, 2, and 4** below. Likes are not final story decisions.

## Current favorites

### 1. Same Goal, Different Methods
They identify a real injustice and might want comparable reforms. Hero refuses cruelty/sacrificing innocents; the Antagonist accepts terrible costs. Shared goal does **not** mean Hero is morally passive.

### 2. The Honorable Bargain
The Antagonist trades land, a kingdom, an item, military access, information or magic to an independent ally for crucial support. He personally honors the agreement. The allied faction harms the Hero's friends/people, and Hero resists. Loyalty among villains can coexist with injustice toward civilians.

### 4. The Hidden Truth
Hero uncovers an essential secret that can undermine, redirect or expose the Villain's objective; both need the same information for opposing purposes.

## Other possibilities (not rejected)

3. Initially shared enemy → temporary cooperation → later ideological break.
5. Both need to protect someone by mutually incompatible methods.
6. Hero and council members have a historical connection.
7. Villain leaves Hero alive for an explicit strategic/psychological/ethical reason, not convenience.

## Escalation sketch (only a possible progression)

- Hero first faces **effects** of the Villain's strategy and the troops of an ally; his battles are frequent and consequential.
- Rivals inside the Villain's **council** react to Hero's interference; Hero's friendships and allies become part of the stakes.
- Rare early direct encounters highlight an enormous power gap. Hero does **not beg**. Villain may dismiss him as insignificant and beneath the dignity of personal combat—**contempt and indifference, not kindness**. His failure to kill the Hero needs no secret fondness or elaborate excuse *while the Hero poses no meaningful threat*. As Hero's influence grows, this rationale must be reevaluated.
- Their beliefs, secret history and objectives become clearer to both.
- In an eventual extreme, genuinely high-stakes clash, **both may fight to kill the other**. Earlier dismissal is gone. Creator prefers that both somehow survive, but the mechanism is still open and must arise from believable events, not a staged death or convenient resurrection.

All sequences above can be re-ordered, combined or replaced once a premise is chosen.

## Villain's rarity rule

A high-ranking council member or an ally's lord can destroy an army, so the Antagonist personally appearing on the battlefield should dramatically change the meaning of the war. Do not make him fight a new mid-level boss every episode.
