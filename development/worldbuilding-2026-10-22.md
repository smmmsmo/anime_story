## 1. Creative identity of the world

**CREATOR SELECTED / STRONGLY STATED**
- A serious, progressive, long-form original **fictional animated fantasy series**, not sci-fi, not historical realism, not childish storytelling.
- Ancient/medieval-era ambience: kings, royals, cities, fortresses, roads, villages, trade, competing kingdoms, magicians, magic, demons, other creatures, monsters, dungeons and dangerous unexplored locations.
- **Ordinary everyday life** coexists with epic conflict: children grow up, merchants trade, farmers work, families study and travel, markets open, guards protect settlements. Constant disasters would cheapen stakes.
- Politics and resource competition are essential; avoid a world existing solely to stage fights.
- Religion need not be a major narrative focus. Prior creator constraints disallow supernatural origins relying on literal gods or deity worship.
- A shared Hunter Guild institution exists for adventurers and monster hunters.
- Study **story, world and characters first**; no animation production or visual design plan yet.

**INSPIRATIONAL REFERENCES (do not copy story, names or lore):** Attack on Titan's steady revelations and emotional weight, Black Clover's kingdom rivalries/protector culture, God of War's intensity and mythical adventure, Jujutsu Kaisen's darker action, Pokémon's creatively named fantastical geography/creatures, anime-style natural civilian lives, Castlevania/Overlord for composed antagonist presence and layers of authority, Street Fighter/Akuma for forbidden-power inspiration, and cinematic South Indian emotional drama.

**CREATOR'S IMPORTANT QUALIFIER:** All seven kingdoms nevertheless have formidable warriors, protectors, generals, troops, mages and capable fighter traditions. No kingdom is unable to wage war or use magic merely because another has that specialty. Kingdom 2 is the **strongest-warriors** center, not a power monopoly.

**CREATOR SELECTED — ANTAGONIST HOMELAND:** The Antagonist is **royal-born in Slot 7**, the elegant prestigious **education / libraries / knowledge / royal-political-power** kingdom. It is a **top-tier important kingdom, NOT the strongest fighter kingdom**. The exact royalty, culture, education methods, school names, vault contents, national symbols and capital city are all TBD. It should have deep knowledge archives, yet not collapse into the different **Advanced Magic** specialty of Slot 6.

The creator selected these specialties but explicitly wants to discuss and potentially refine their **details** later.

## 3. Kingdom interdependence and politics

**CREATOR STRONGLY LIKED**
- Nine isolated self-sufficient superpowers would be boring; with seven, **tradeoffs and mutual dependence** are central.
- Example: top fighters can benefit from another country's magical weaponmaking; a naval blockade can cripple international commerce of rich farmlands; rare healing remedies from the medicine kingdom may determine another army's survival.
- Rivals can still trade, form alliances, restrict resources, spy, compete for elite prestige, dispute borders and fight over minerals, medicines, maps, relics and routes.
- Political structures do not have to match. A royal court, military order, scholarly council, merchants and intelligence networks may pull different strings.
- Each kingdom can have generals, elite guard units and protector institutions. Military schools, command titles, constitutional structure, diplomacy and kingdom-specific uniforms are **unselected details**.

## 4. World geography

**CREATOR LIKED the following broad layout, NOT an official map**
- **Northern** mountains, cold passes, fortresses and harsh frontier.
- **Central** fertile river valley, major population and trade corridors.
- **Eastern** mineral ranges, mining and ancient ruins.
- **Western** forests, waterways and unusual creatures.
- **Southern** seas, islands, shipping hubs and occasional pirates.
- **Remote / outlying** dangerous wastelands, ruined cities, demon-haunted areas and dungeons.

Borders and geography should influence commerce, governance, war and travel. Not every region belongs to a kingdom: independent cities, abandoned territories, risky neutral lands and non-human domains should remain possible. A ruined city claimed by creatures with rare mining deposits could trigger competing kingdom expeditions, for example (**just an example**).

Names, cardinal placement and travel times for the seven kingdoms, continent names and histories remain undecided.

**NOT ADOPTED:** The Seventh Oath / Black Divide's original lore, sacrifice-based barrier and origin story. The Seventh Oath was a **liked candidate narrative**, not what the new seven-kingdom count automatically means.

## 5. Guilds, Hunters, Protectors

**CREATOR SELECTED:** A **Hunter Guild / Guild System** exists. Professional adventurers and hunters undertake work in the world.

**LIKED PROPOSALS, RULES NOT YET FINAL**
- Contract board and tasks such as monster hunting, dungeon clearing/exploration, rare material gathering, rescuing missing citizens, escorting merchant caravans and protecting villages.
- Separate (though interoperating) systems: royal armies / Kingdom Protectors fulfill state military roles; Hunters take specialized guild work.
- Guild branches may appear in multiple cities/kingdoms. This produces new trade, recognition, neutrality and political problems, but cross-border law needs a later decision.
- Hunter grades, guild registration, rank insignia, party composition, rewards, sanctions and whether Hunters can join Royal Guard are all TBD.
- **Hunter rank need not equal innate power rank**; these may be independent measurements. No tiers selected.

## 6. Creatures, Demons and Dungeons

**WORLD INGREDIENTS SELECTED; SPECIFIC LORE NOT SELECTED**
- Diverse wild creatures, monsters and demons of unequal intelligence and motives.
- Demons / stronger bosses / ancient entities may have kingdoms/territories, followers, rare knowledge, goals and negotiable relationships.
- Dungeons, ruins, rare items and dangerous hunting regions form part of adventuring life.
- Some creatures can be bargained with, befriended or bonded rather than only slain.
- Hero or Antagonist might receive new knowledge, abilities, weapons or power through encounters, battles, bonds or contracts; **not automatic kill = random magic loot**.
- Specific demon hierarchy, species names, boss encounters, ancient disaster, seals and dungeon categories are undecided.

## 7. Power growth and magic — brainstorming, not a system yet

**IDEAS ONLY**
- People may have different **powers at birth** that awaken or expand with age, training and dangerous experiences.
- A formal power assessment, recognition or registration at a certain age; maybe rank/level systems. No ages or institutions chosen.
- Layers or levels of growth, with a rare exceptional **apex level beyond usual tiers**. Antagonist might attain this rare tier while the hero remains several below in raw strength, but ranks and quantities TBD.
- Fighting, training, guild quests and survival lead to development. Mage, melee warrior, healer and other specialties overlap.
- Rare rings, ornaments, ancient swords and relics may enhance capacity through wearing, bonding or *integration*. Mechanics and drawbacks TBD.
- Demons, bosses, trials, pacts, bargaining and alliances could grant unusual knowledge or strength.
- **Dark / forbidden pathway** resembling Akuma-like power pursuit may grant immense strength but damage mental/emotional judgment. The Antagonist may use *both* regular and dark paths; the Hero may avoid dark methods. The Antagonist must remain a moral agent with intelligible decisions, **not merely mind-controlled into evil**.
- The Hero's discipline, public goodwill, friendship and positive reputation may help him persevere, access allies or develop power; not necessarily literal magical public-opinion fuel.
- Hard limits, price, energy, fatigue, exact tiers, rank grades, regeneration, corruption, genetic components and exceptional power ceilings all TBD.

## 8. Hero and Antagonist — earlier exploration, no fixed biography

**WORKING PREFERENCES**
- Two leads of approximately similar age; an Antagonist **1–2 years older** is a possibility, not chosen chronology.
- Hero: initially fairly normal/naive, good-hearted and justice-oriented, capable of strong action and hard decisions; some born power which grows; friends, mentors and possible romance. Travels between towns and kingdoms, meets hunters and solves smaller threats before discovering wider stakes.
- Antagonist: **royal-born** in Slot 7, raised with access to education, influence and libraries; initially kind, intelligent, talented, composed and mannered, **not monstrously powerful from infancy**. A childhood crisis may alter his path. He eventually becomes far above the hero in raw power and feared by rulers, but is not necessarily the strongest being who exists.
- He favors calculated decisions, composure, articulate behavior and respected agreements. He is not a loud, impulsive brute. Council, lieutenants, exceptional fighters, armies and independent allies carry out significant parts of his schemes. His *own* fights are rare and consequential.
- His motives should be understandable even if he commits serious wrongs. Dark practices can amplify his corruption without excusing it.
- Early in conflict, his overwhelming strength may cause him to disregard the Hero as beneath his notice, **not out of pity or mercy**. Once the Hero becomes threatening, this rationale cannot remain indefinite.
- Both may truly intend lethal action in an extreme final confrontation, though the creator currently prefers **both leads surviving** by a plausible mechanism; no ending is decided.
- Romantic and family relationships, friendship, fear, grief and loyalty should be real sources of tension and change.

## 9. Chronology and potential first crossover

**ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY — DO NOT TURN INTO SET EPISODES**
1. Begin narrative **before both births**, establish the seven-kingdom setting, ordinary politics, families and everyday life.
2. Hero and Antagonist born roughly near each other in time, possibly a small age gap. Childhood normalcy has local conflicts.
3. At some uncertain childhood age, a devastating incident may launch the Antagonist's transformation and long largely-offscreen ascent.
4. Hero might develop powers around **11–15**, join or encounter Guilds, take part in hunts/quests and travel heavily through the later teen years (perhaps **15–18**).
5. The Antagonist may return at a much higher rank around the time the Hero is **18** (possibly Antagonist **20–21**), depending on the final chosen age difference.
6. A future plot could bring the Hero into a town where a *subordinate of the true Antagonist* attempts to seize something. Hero confronts the subordinate. This connects local journeys with the global antagonist without prematurely requiring a direct Hero–Antagonist duel.

The exact first meeting, fight result, ages, travel duration, pacing, locations and episode count are **all open**. No fixed five-phase structural template.
