# Teaser 01 — 60-Second Animation Production Pipeline

> **Status:** PLANNED · **Current working phase:** Phase 1 — Project Foundation (not approved/finished yet)  
> **Purpose:** Learn the end-to-end animation process by completing one real 60-second animated proof of concept.  
> **Scope:** Production experiment only. This document **does not finalize story canon, character designs, kingdom layouts, names, events, or future episodes**. For canon read [../../development/decisions.md](../../development/decisions.md) and [../../STATUS.md](../../STATUS.md).  
> **Last updated:** 2026-10-22

## 0. Start here: what we are actually making

আমরা একটি **one-minute world-centric ensemble fantasy teaser** বানাচ্ছি। এটা শুধু Hero/Villain showcase নয়। **Kingdom, ordinary people, side characters/NPCs, cultures, daily activity, atmosphere, conflict, Hero ও Antagonist—সবাই যেন meaningful presence পায়।** Equal narrative dignity **does not** require equal screen time or equal shot length. একটি NPC-এর ২ সেকেন্ডের meaningful action, তার জীবনের ইঙ্গিত, বা নিজস্ব choice তাকে একটি decorative crowd figure-এর চেয়ে অনেক বেশি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ করতে পারে।

**Main goals**
1. A complete, watchable **60-second** animated video from brief to export.
2. Run through actual pre-production → production → post-production, not a still-image slideshow.
3. Test both **limited animation** and at least one **genuine drawn motion segment**, to measure difficulty.
4. Learn how long **each task and each shot** really takes and what must change before episodes.
5. Keep the teaser's chosen images, scenes, and character designs **provisional** until separately approved for the story.

**Provisional delivery specification** (may change only with explicit Phase-1 decision):
- Canvas: **1920 × 1080**, landscape 16:9.
- Edit/export timeline: **24 fps**, progressive.
- Animate on **twos (12 unique drawings/sec)** where appropriate; held frames, loops, and varied spacing are allowed.
- Sound target: stereo, 48 kHz; final delivery: **H.264 MP4**; keep project files and a high-quality master if practical.
- Target runtime: **~60 s**, credits/title included.
- **7–14 days is only a stretch target**, not an estimate or deadline. Log actual hours; quality/learning over artificial speed.

### Non-negotiable creative and production principles
- **Living-world test:** show signs of ordinary routines, trades, politics, guilds, and local stakes—not just empty castles.
- **Every visible character has a reason to exist:** an occupation, desire, reaction, task, or consequence. Don't call all civilians “unimportant NPCs.”
- **Unequal screen time is fine:** time distribution follows the rhythm and story function of each shot, **not a fixed equality quota**.
- **Show, don't lecture:** no rushed lore dump, full reveal of every kingdom, or mandatory introduction of the entire cast.
- **AI assists concept sketches / reference / color studies**; the creator remains in charge of selecting designs, cleaning, coloring, keyframing, and actual animation. Keep character consistency and editability.
- **One phase at a time** with explicit exit criteria, but allow deliberate returns to earlier phases when a test proves a change is needed.
- **Nothing enters canon automatically.** `PRODUCTION LOCK` means stable *for this teaser*, not locked story/world lore.
- Animation effort is not even across shots. Sample different workload levels intentionally.

## PHASE 1 — Project Foundation / Direction Lock

**Goal:** Stop scope creep before visual production.

**Work**
- Write the teaser's one-sentence pitch, why this test exists, and the feeling it should leave.
- Decide tone: serious, cinematic, dark medieval-feeling fantasy, alive and emotionally credible, not childish/comedic.
- Decide whether the immediate goal is *private learning only* or also *audience-facing*; do not build a marketing campaign by default.
- Confirm target length, aspect ratio, FPS, animation approach, delivery format, language/subtitles if any.
- Choose an intentionally **small scope**: a few meaningful people/locations, some atmospheric shots, and one real animation challenge.
- Decide a **reveal policy**: mystery and suggestive glimpses rather than complete plot, powers, exact politics, final story beats.
- Define a rough time budget **by effort per shot**, not equal seconds per category. Keep 20–30% contingency as a planning option, not a promise.

**Required**
- [ ] One-sentence objective and audience/use case
- [ ] Tone/mood words and boundaries
- [ ] Runtime, dimensions, timeline FPS, final format
- [ ] Teaser vs story-canon distinction acknowledged
- [ ] Explicit **out of scope** list (whole episode, full lore, full cast, long fight, publishing campaign)
- [ ] Creator confirms brief

**Deliverable:** `brief.md` or Phase-1 section in a working journal.  
**Do NOT:** write entire season, finish everyone’s outfit, pick unapproved lore as canon, buy tools before the workflow is tested.  
**Exit gate:** creator can explain *what the teaser is, what it is not, and what the finished file will be*.

## PHASE 2 — Content Architecture / Living-World Coverage

**Goal:** Make the teaser world-rich without crowding 60 seconds.

**Coverage buckets** — everyone matters; **not equal-duration slots**:

| Pillar | Show by action rather than exposition | Example only — NOT canon |
|---|---|---|
| Kingdom / geography | Scale, architecture, social contrast | skyline, bridge, port, distant citadel |
| Civilian / NPC life | Occupation, choices, hardship, joy, routine | vendor closing a stall, medic assisting someone |
| Guild / local institution | How people earn, protect, or organize | hunter accepting a notice, patrol briefing |
| Hero | Human viewpoint and agency | reacts to danger, helps someone, moves toward a goal |
| Antagonist | Presence, consequence, authority, mystery | restrained glance, silent arrival, aftermath |
| Threat / political tension | Stakes beyond one duel | alarm, disrupted trade, suspicious procession |
| Emotional / symbolic close | Final feeling and memorable image | human reaction, emblem, unresolved consequence |

**Work**
- Propose a **shortlist** of locations and people worth showing (including people who aren't protagonists).
- Give each selected NPC a specific purpose: *what are they doing; why does it matter; what does it reveal?*
- Define 1–2 recognizable cultural/social cues per shown location.
- Indicate which story/kingdom specifics are **TBD** rather than inventing them.
- Create a **reveal inventory:** show, imply, or hide for each idea.

**Required**
- [ ] All must-have content categories represented in plan
- [ ] At least one **meaningful NPC/civilian action**, not just passive crowd filler
- [ ] Locations and people shortlist manageable in one minute
- [ ] No need to represent every kingdom or every character
- [ ] Visibility hierarchy supports rhythm, not artificial numerical equality

**Deliverable:** `content-map.md`.  
**Do NOT:** allocate ten seconds to everyone just to appear fair; confuse background population with agency.  
**Exit gate:** creator approves *which kinds of lives and forces* the teaser will show.

## PHASE 3 — Beat Sheet / Emotional Spine

**Goal:** One-minute teaser should have a beginning, build, and final emotional hook even without revealing plot.

**Possible beats (suggested order, NOT mandatory/canon):**
1. **Orientation** — where/what kind of world is this?
2. **Human life** — let us feel people living in it.
3. **Individual agency** — Hero and/or another meaningful person taking action.
4. **Wider stakes** — kingdom, institution, trade, fear or political dimension.
5. **Mysterious power** — Antagonist or a consequence of someone influential.
6. **Escalation** — an event/reaction/action briefly changes the rhythm.
7. **Aftertaste** — emotional question, striking final tableau, optional working title.

**Work**
- Draft 1–2 sentences per beat and the intended emotion.
- Mark major audio transitions: silence → ambiance → rising energy → punctuation.
- Roughly distribute time but **do not lock seconds yet**.
- Ensure scenes imply a connected world and aren't random unrelated wallpaper.
- Let the hero/antagonist appear in service of the teaser, not dominate by default.

**Required**
- [ ] Clear emotional starting point
- [ ] Living-world moment with consequence or activity
- [ ] Escalation/contrast of energy
- [ ] Strong final image/emotion
- [ ] No forced exposition of unresolved plot

**Deliverable:** `beat-sheet.md`.  
**Do NOT:** force the full series into a 60-second plot or treat this as an episode script.  
**Exit gate:** beats can be described simply without narrating all the world's history.

## PHASE 4 — Visual Exploration, AI References, Style Tests

**Goal:** Find a **cohesive visual language** fast, before expensive drawing.

**Exploration tracks**
- **World:** silhouettes of castle/city/village/road, architecture, landscape, weather, time of day, depth layers.
- **People:** Hero, Antagonist, selected NPCs (age impression, shape, clothing hierarchy, occupation/readability), no exhaustive cast sheet.
- **Objects:** symbols, tools, weapons, guild insignia, banners **only if shot-relevant**.
- **Rendering:** line weight, coloring strategy, shadow simplicity, background detail and readability on small screens.
- **Movement feasibility:** at least one tiny sample for walk/gesture, hair/cloth, expression or action.
- **Consistency:** multi-view / pose-to-pose similarity, scale, colors, repeatability.

**AI reference workflow**
1. Write a **specific, original** reference brief for each asset.
2. Generate **multiple rough black-and-white sketch options**; choose the strongest design.
3. If useful, generate **color explorations using that selected sketch as reference**.
4. Make a clean, editable **master reference sheet**: silhouette, face anchors, major shapes, limited palette, proportions.
5. **Manually correct** anatomy, costumes, perspective and model consistency; AI output is a draft/reference, **not automatically a final production frame**.
6. Test the same design at 2–3 poses/angles; if consistency fails, simplify it before locking.
7. Record source/licensing and don't imitate a specific existing franchise's designs; inspirations are about feel, not copied characters.

**Required**
- [ ] Small moodboard (selected, not hundreds of images)
- [ ] Line-art / coloring sample
- [ ] At least one consistent main-character reference
- [ ] At least one civilian/NPC design style sample
- [ ] At least one environment depth/layering test
- [ ] One quick movement feasibility test or clearly flagged risk
- [ ] Chosen style achievable with available time and skill

**Deliverable:** `visual-direction/` concept/reference exports + short style notes.  
**Do NOT:** finalize entire world encyclopedia, produce hundreds of detailed designs, assume color generation fixes line mistakes.  
**Exit gate:** selected test drawings look like they could belong to the same production.

## PHASE 5 — Provisional Asset & Dependency Breakdown

**Goal:** List what needs to exist; **provisional until animatic lock**.

**Organize by SHOT and by REUSABLE ASSET** rather than a random art wishlist.

| Category | Typical examples | Reuse strategy |
|---|---|---|
| BG | skyline, street, hall, terrain, sky | layered background; camera moves |
| Characters | selected Hero/Villain/NPC references | shared proportions/palettes, reusable poses |
| Motion | blink, walk, gesture, reaction, short attack | loop or unique sequence where justified |
| Props | sign, tool, weapon, banner, artifact | modular independent layer |
| FX | smoke, dust, water, sparks, weather | loop/overlay, not every effect custom |
| Audio | ambience, footsteps, impacts, whoosh, music | source list, reusable library |
| Editorial | working title, shot slates, simple typography | template |

**Work**
- Assign **Shot ID** (`SH010`, `SH020`, etc.).
- Note for each: BG, characters, props, motion, FX, sound, and dependencies.
- Label priority: **MUST / SHOULD / OPTIONAL**.
- Label animation complexity: **A = still + camera**, **B = limited loop/gesture**, **C = expressive multi-pose**, **D = genuine dense/action hand-drawn segment**. The goal is a *mix*, not 60 seconds of level D.
- Plan 1–2 throwaway tests early to uncover unexpected difficulty.
- Prefer reusable assets without making everyone look identical.

**Required**
- [ ] Assets linked to specific shots
- [ ] No asset with no clear purpose unless marked optional
- [ ] Complexity and key risks marked per shot
- [ ] Reusability opportunities recorded
- [ ] Provisional workload is small enough to attempt

**Deliverable:** `asset-inventory.md` (or spreadsheet), with shot dependencies.  
**Do NOT:** begin final asset painting yet; the animatic may cut scenes.  
**Exit gate:** each proposed shot has an understandable path to completion.

## PHASE 6 — Storyboard

**Goal:** Establish **readable visual storytelling** without polishing drawings.

**Each storyboard panel records:**
- Shot ID, panel number, rough composition, location and characters.
- Action / actor's intention (especially civilians/NPCs).
- Lens/framing description (wide/medium/close, not fake technical precision).
- Camera direction: static, pan, tilt, push, track.
- Estimated length in seconds (changeable).
- Transition and rough sound note.
- Animation level A/B/C/D and biggest implementation challenge.

**Required**
- [ ] Whole 60s roughly boarded
- [ ] Shot numbering consistent
- [ ] Distinct visual scale/angles to avoid monotony
- [ ] Civilians/side characters are intentional, not decorations
- [ ] Motion directions and cuts readable
- [ ] At least one actual hand-drawn motion segment planned

**Deliverable:** `storyboard/` sequential panels and a shot list.  
**Do NOT:** spend production time cleaning, rendering, or full-coloring the boards.  
**Exit gate:** someone unfamiliar with the project can follow the flow from ordered panels.

## PHASE 7 — Animatic / Editorial Timing Test

**Goal:** Test the story, rhythm and shot durations **before detailed animation**.

**Work**
1. Place storyboard frames in an editor in shot order.
2. Set the timeline to 24 fps; adjust shot times until around 60 seconds.
3. Add temporary camera moves, basic fades/cuts, scratch SFX/music/ambience (check final licensing later).
4. Watch with sound, watch on mute, then watch at phone size.
5. Ask: Which parts feel alive? Which feel like empty scenic filler? Does an NPC choice read clearly? Does Hero/Villain presence overpower world diversity? Is the ending memorable?
6. Revise cuts and **remove/reuse assets** before committing to final drawings.

**Required**
- [ ] Entire animatic plays start to finish
- [ ] Approx 60 s total including title if used
- [ ] All planned shots have IDs and timing
- [ ] Clear escalation and pacing
- [ ] Every surviving shot justifies its seconds
- [ ] Creator approves moving into production lock

**Deliverable:** `animatic-v01.mp4` + ordered edit notes.  
**Do NOT:** confuse a temporary still-video animatic with the **final fully completed teaser**.  
**Exit gate:** a watchable timed edit exists that does not need major structural changes.

## PHASE 8 — Teaser Production / Design Lock

**Goal:** Decide exactly what gets finished. This is **not a lore/canon lock**.

**Lock sheet**
- Final shot order + shot IDs; trim timings/frames.
- Per shot: who is visible, who moves, BG plate/layers, action, FX, camera, sound requirements.
- Selected master references: key proportions, silhouette, line style and palette.
- Which shots are A/B/C/D; reserve the most effort for one meaningful difficult sample.
- Edit version / reference IDs, filenames and revision practices.
- MUST vs OPTIONAL cuts if schedule slips.
- Rights status: soundtrack/SFX/fonts/AI assets and permission for public release, if planned.

**Required**
- [ ] Locked shot list references a particular animatic version
- [ ] Every shot has a feasible technique
- [ ] Final **teaser-specific** designs chosen
- [ ] Asset inventory updated after cuts (Phase-5 draft revised)
- [ ] Critical unknowns resolved or explicitly listed as risks
- [ ] Creator approves start of production

**Deliverable:** `production-lock.md` with approved shot list, style and dependencies.  
**Do NOT:** add unrelated lore, cast, or kingdom content after lock; use a controlled change note if necessary.  
**Exit gate:** there is no mystery about what must be drawn, animated, colored and assembled.

## PHASE 9 — Actual Production (shot-by-shot)

Instead of finishing **all backgrounds, then all drawings**, it may be faster for a beginner to finish **one representative test shot first**, validate the pipeline, and then batch similar work. Keep the approved shot IDs throughout.

### 9A — Backgrounds / environments
- Create final compositions and editable depth layers (foreground/midground/background/sky).
- Preserve 16:9 safe framing; establish light direction and atmospheric perspective.
- Reuse sky, architecture modules, crowds only where visually appropriate.
- For human-centered scenes, background must support character action rather than bury it.

**Output:** ready BG plates/layers. **Check:** depth and readability hold up in motion.

### 9B — Production-ready character/prop assets
- Produce only shot-needed views of Hero, Antagonist, meaningful NPCs, costumes and objects.
- Retain reference for consistent proportions, facial markers, clothing shapes and palette.
- Give recurring NPCs distinct identities/actions; for distant crowds, simplify responsibly.
- Keep layered source files editable; no need for full turnaround when only 3/4 view appears.

**Output:** final per-shot models/assets. **Check:** character consistency across selected views.

### 9C — Rough animation, keys, timing
- Plan **key poses** first; compare silhouette, spacing, anticipation, follow-through, staging.
- For action, rough animate a few seconds before cleanup to learn true time cost.
- Reuse limited cycles where appropriate: walks, blinks, breathing, cloak/hair, crowds.
- **Frame rate note:** 60 × 24 = 1,440 VIDEO frames; it **does not** mean 1,440 distinct drawings. Animating on twos or holds reduces unique drawings, but highly active shots still need careful motion.

**Output:** rough animated shots at approved timing. **Check:** readable movement without color.

### 9D — In-betweens, cleanup, color
- Add only motion in-betweens actually needed for pleasing timing.
- Clean up line quality and proportion drift.
- Add flat color; apply consistent shadow style / limited highlights.
- Use saved palettes/paint-fill helpers/AI color references as aids, but inspect **every shot** for incorrect fills, hands, faces, flicker and costume inconsistency.
- Watch sequences, not just attractive individual frames.

**Output:** colored animation layers/shot passes. **Check:** consistent character identity and clean motion.

### 9E — Compositing / camera / FX
- Composite character, BG depth layers, props, particles, weather, lighting and camera moves.
- Use 2D parallax, fog, subtle grading, blur sparingly to give depth.
- Favor meaningful moving elements over noisy filters; protect face/hand readability.
- Keep the important darker scenes *legible*, not crushed to black.

**Output:** numbered finished shot renders (`SH010_v01`, etc.). **Check:** shots match animatic duration and style.

**Phase-9 minimum pass criteria**
- [ ] Each locked shot rendered at expected resolution, FPS, and length
- [ ] Character proportions/colors remain consistent
- [ ] At least one true frame-by-frame/multi-pose hand-drawn movement test included
- [ ] Meaningful civilian/NPC action survives into the actual moving video
- [ ] No major broken layers, weird poses, flicker or unintended empty frames
- [ ] Final shot folder matches locked shot list, or exceptions documented

## PHASE 10 — Sound, Edit, QA, Export

**Editorial**
- Replace rough animatic footage with exported shots; preserve shot IDs/version history.
- Tune cuts, contrast and pace; don't hide weak animation behind excessive transitions.
- Insert title/working logo only if wanted; no need to finalize the entire series branding.

**Sound**
- Choose appropriately licensed soundtrack or original score.
- Layer world ambience (street, market, wind, crowd, metal, steps) to make places believable.
- Place character/impact/transition SFX intentionally; use silence where helpful.
- Balance music with effects/any dialogue; avoid clipping, harsh jumps and unwanted loudness.
- Maintain a `sources-and-licenses.md` log for externally sourced audio/visuals before public distribution.

**Quality assurance**
- [ ] Starts and ends cleanly; correct ~60s total
- [ ] Final output 1920×1080, 24 fps, stereo audio (or explicitly revised settings)
- [ ] No blank frames, missing layers, compositing borders or accidental watermarks
- [ ] Colors consistent from shot to shot and dark scenes still visible
- [ ] No broken character or crowd continuity
- [ ] Audio synced, balanced, no digital clipping
- [ ] Watch once on a laptop/desktop AND once at phone size with headphones
- [ ] Verify credits/licensing if sharing publicly
- [ ] Master/source projects backed up; keep a final exported copy

**Deliverables**
- `teaser-01_final_v01.mp4`
- Optional high-quality master and/or individual shot exports
- Editable scene files, reference sheets, soundtrack license notes, final project
- Final export settings and problems-to-fix (if any) in a short changelog

**Exit gate:** a complete, reviewed MP4 plays reliably start to finish.

## PHASE 11 — Postmortem / Episode Planning Evidence

**This is the key research output of the entire experiment.** Estimate future episodes from **actual logged effort**, not naive multiplying 60 seconds × 20.

**Record**
- Time for each phase AND each shot, including corrections and rework.
- Number of frames/drawings, shot seconds, motion complexity A/B/C/D, reusability.
- Percentage of finished duration using still+camera vs limited vs dense animation.
- Most time-consuming steps: selecting AI sketch, fixing consistency, coloring, in-betweens, backgrounds, effects, sound, export.
- What tools/shortcuts helped; what shortcuts caused additional cleanup.
- Assets reusable for the eventual show and which are teaser-only.
- Quality compromises, fixes and learning goals for next practice.

**Useful metrics** (estimates, not promises):
- `hours per completed shot` by complexity.
- `animation-hours / animated seconds` for level B/C/D, **reported separately**.
- `total hours = pre-production + production + post + rework`.
- `first-time overhead` vs `repeatable production`; distinguish one-time character setup, backgrounds and reusable cycles.
- For an episode estimate: plan shot count and **mix of complexity levels** first; include dialogue, continuity, revisions, asset reuse, crew size, sound and contingency.

**Questions to answer**
- Which animation technique will we keep for the series?
- Which shot type is too costly for the value it adds?
- Where does AI save actual hours—and where does correction erase the gain?
- What can we reuse without making the world feel repetitive?
- What should the next short test investigate before committing to an episode?

**Deliverable:** `retrospective.md` including a revised pipeline and evidence-backed time ranges.

### Example visual rhythms (only examples, never settled scenes)

One possible set of cuts: big kingdom exterior → a worker/vendor/medic doing something consequential → Hero responding to a local event → guild/public institution → signs of kingdom-scale tension → Antagonist glimpse → brief original action/gesture animation → a personal/emotional reaction → quiet final image/title.

**Not a timetable**: individual shots can last 1, 3, 7 or 12 seconds if the animation, emotional weight, and pacing justify it. **No equal time allocation is required.**

## 3. Animation complexity and effort strategy

| Level | What moves? | Best for | Cost/risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Camera/held art | Pan, zoom, parallax on limited artwork | Scale, atmosphere, geography | Low motion-drawing cost; risk of slideshow feel |
| B — Limited | Blink, hand, fabric, breathing, crowd loop | Believable lives and mood | Moderate asset setup; repeatability |
| C — Expressive | Turn, gesture, short walk, reaction | Agency and emotion | Consistency/in-between work |
| D — Dense / action | Fast change of poses, short impact or running | True animation challenge and peak | High cost; restrict to a short purposeful test |

**Recommended experiment, not mandatory quotas:** use A/B for world breadth, B/C for meaningful people/NPCs, and at least one C/D test that genuinely stretches drawing/timing skill. Don't equate “beautiful still” with “animation finished.”

## 6. Tool choices are optional; workflow comes first

Choose **one** practical tool per main task at first, not seven competing apps:
- **Sketch/paint/reference:** Krita, Clip Studio Paint, or another layer-friendly drawing app.
- **2D frame animation:** Krita animation timeline, OpenToonz, Blender Grease Pencil, or comparable software.
- **Compositing/camera:** Blender, DaVinci Resolve/Fusion or comparable layer/video compositor.
- **Video/audio editing:** DaVinci Resolve, Kdenlive/Shotcut, Audacity, etc.
- **Planning:** Markdown in this repo + simple duration/time table.

These are candidates, **not a required stack**. Choose after checking the Mac workflow and doing one tiny end-to-end shot test. AI sketch/color-reference services are helpers, not substitutes for the creator's intended hands-on animation.
