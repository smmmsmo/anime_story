# Factions, Institutions & Alliances

_Updated 2026-10-22; confirmed framework and open mechanics distinguished._

## Hunter Guilds — accepted as a world institution

The creator explicitly asked for a **Guild System / Hunters** alongside the kingdoms.

- Hunter guilds and/or branches exist to organize adventuring work, hunting and dangerous assignments.
- Candidate missions: monster/demon hunting, dungeon exploration, caravan protection, search-and-rescue and rare material collection.
- The distinction between **guild-affiliated hunters** and **royal armies/elite protectors** is important. They need not have identical ranks or authorities.
- Registration, branch jurisdiction, contracts, guild politics, hunter rank scale, legal status and paths between hunter service and royal duty were suggested; mechanics **not yet locked**.
- Guilds can span borders, introducing tension with local kingdoms, restrictions, trade and alliances. Cross-border policy is undecided.

## Kingdom and military organizations

Every kingdom has its own capable soldiers, strong warriors and magic users, with elites/protectors/generals. Kingdom military structure and specialist roles need not be standardized.

## Antagonist's hierarchy & independent allies (earlier working preference)

His proposed network: antagonist at apex → inner council → lieutenants and powerful subordinates → organized troops. External sovereign rulers, creatures, experts and factions may cooperate by negotiation, not automatically as obedient underlings.

Allies may demand territories, rare knowledge, safety, access, political recognition, resources, enhancements or reciprocal assistance. The antagonist should not habitually betray his partners; deals can be costly to innocent bystanders, allowing moral conflict with the hero.

**No roster, command ranks, alliance treaty or guild rule is canon.**
